# <u>Galileo's mission</u> 
><span style="color: white">**Introduction**</span>

Galileo was a NASA unmanned spacecraft that aimed to study the planet Jupiter and its moons. Just like the Italian mathematician and astronomer Galileo Galilei had once done. 

The mission was very successful and yielded groundbreaking discoveries about the Jovian system.

><span style="color: white">**Overview**</span>

The Galileo probe was launched on October 18, 1989 aboard the Space Shuttle Atlantis from Florida, USA. The journey to Jupiter took six years, during which the probe used gravitational pendulums past Venus and twice past Earth to gain speed. 

In December 1995, Galileo arrived at Jupiter:
* Just before arrival, a descent probe was lowered into Jupiter's atmosphere to collect data. The probe measured extreme winds of up to 720 km/h, found that the atmosphere was drier than expected and as a result recorded fewer lightning events than predicted. 
* While the main craft remained in orbit as an orbiter.
* Originally, Galileo made three flybys of Europe. The mission was extended and eight additional flybys were made in different altitudes ranging from 196 to 3582 kilometers above the surface. 

After almost 14 years in space, the mission came to an end. The probe was intentionally sent into Jupiter's atmosphere on September 21, 2003, where it burned up. This was done to prevent the probe (possibly contaminated with terrestrial microbes) from crashing into the moon Europa and affecting a potential alien ecosystem. 

><span style="color: white">**Key discoveries**</span>

One of the most important discoveries was the strong evidence for a saltwater ocean under the ice crust of the moon Europa. A detailed study of the ice surface of this moon could be made.

Galileo captured detailed images of active volcanism on the moon Io. Images were made of lava fountains, similar to Hawaiian volcanoes, but on a much larger scale.

The probe also studied Jupiter's intense magnetic field and its complex system of rings and moons.

# <u>The Cassini-Huygens project</u> 
><span style="color: white">**Introduction**</span>

The Cassini-Huygens project was an extremely successful, joint unmanned space mission of the American Space Agency (NASA), the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Italian Space Agency (ASI) to explore the Saturn system and its moons in detail. 

The mission consisted of two main parts:
* The Cassini orbiter. The mothership orbiting Saturn and studying the planet, its rings and numerous moons.
* The Huygens probe. A lander designed to descend into the atmosphere and land on the surface of Titan, Saturn's largest moon. 

><span style="color: white">**Overview**</span>

The probe, the mothership (Cassini) with the lander attached to it (Huygens), was launched on October 15, 1997.

After a journey of almost seven years, the probe entered orbit around Saturn in July 2004.

In January 2005, the Huygens lander successfully descended through Titan's thick, nitrogen-rich atmosphere and landed on its surface. It was the first (and so far only) landing on a moon in the outer solar system.

The Cassini orbiter remained active around Saturn for more than 13 years, much longer than the originally planned four-year mission, sending a wealth of data back to Earth.

On September 15, 2017, the Cassini orbiter performed a controlled dive into Saturn's atmosphere, where the intense heat and pressure destroyed the probe. This prevented Cassini from floating around uncontrollably and potentially contaminating the moons Titan or Enceladus (with potentially habitable environments). 

><span style="color: white">**Most important discoveries of the Huygens lander**</span>

This revealed that Titan has an active weather system. Complete with lakes, rivers and seas of liquid methane and ethane, similar to the water cycle on Earth. Evidence was also found for an underground ocean of water and ammonia. The latter must be written on Cassini's hat (see below).

The Huygens lander was built by the ESA and was designed as a shell structure to withstand the extreme conditions of the descent:
* It had a diameter of 2.7 meters.
* It consisted of an outer heat shield for protection during atmospheric intrusion and a descent module containing six scientific instruments.
* After entering the atmosphere, she used a series of three parachutes to slow down its speed and allow a controlled descent of about 2.5 hours.
* She had no communication possibilities of her own with the earth. She sent the data to the Cassini orbiter, which then sent it to Earth.
* She worked for 2 hours and 27 minutes during the descent and another 72 minutes on the surface of Titan. After that, the Cassini orbiter disappeared out of range. 

Titan, a world previously hidden under a dense orange haze, revealed many secrets. Despite the atmospheric haze, the cameras on board were able to take clear images of the surface:
* For example, there was an earth-like landscape with loose sand or mud, composed of water ice pebbles. The landscape showed erosion patterns, drainage channels and coastlines.
* Everything pointed to the presence of liquid methane or ethane on the surface. Two simple compounds from natural gas that are liquid or gaseous at temperatures of -170 degrees. Although the rivers and lakes were dry at the time, methane would have rained down on Saturn's moon not long ago.
* An active cycle of these fluids was established. Rain, rivers and lakes, evaporation, clouds.
* Complex organic molecules were identified in the nitrogen-rich atmosphere.
* The temperature on the surface was determined to be an icy -170 degrees Celsius and the atmospheric pressure was about 1.5 times that of the Earth. 

Titan suddenly became a fascinating, active world with a unique chemistry and geology.

><span style="color: white">**Key discoveries of the Cassini orbiter**</span>

This main satellite brought data across three sub-regions: the moon Titan, the moon Enceladus and the ring structure of Saturn.
Cassini performed several flybys along the moon Titan and measured gravity with great precision. Variations in gravity and the way Titan responds to Saturn's tidal forces showed that the surface and crust move independently of each other. This indicates a liquid layer (the ocean) between the icy crust and the solid core. Scientists suspect that this ocean is made up of a mixture of water and ammonia, which helps keep the water liquid at Titan's extremely low temperatures. 

Cassini discovered spectacular water geysers spewing from the south pole of the moon Enceladus in 2005  . The discovery of these plumes led to a shift in mission priorities, with additional flybys being added to analyze their composition. Cassini has flown through those plumes of water several times:
* The plumes were found to contain chemical compounds considered essential for life, such as methane, carbon dioxide, ammonia and salts.
* Thus the existence of an underground saltwater ocean with hydrothermal activity was confirmed. 
* This made Enceladus one of the most promising places for finding extraterrestrial life in our solar system.

Cassini provided unprecedented details about Saturn 's ring structure and the interaction between the rings and the moons:
* Our static image of the rings has been adjusted to a dynamic and active system.
* Cassini provided the most detailed images of the rings ever. 
* The orbiter revealed thousands of individual small rings, gaps and unexpected structures that are constantly changing.
* Small moons move through the rings and influence them through their gravity, creating edges and openings.
* Spectrometer data confirmed that the rings are mostly pure water ice, ranging in size from microscopic dust particles to chunks the size of a house.
* The data suggest that the rings are relatively young, possibly only a few tens or hundreds of millions of years old. This instead of the age of Saturn itself, being 4.5 billion years.
* Saturn interacts with its rings. Due to its magnetic field, charged water ice particles and other chemical elements rain down from the rings into the planet's upper atmosphere. The phenomenon of "ring rain" was born.
* The rings are classified mainly according to their distance from the planet, their density and their history of discovery. Seven main rings have been identified, they are indicated by letters of the alphabet.
