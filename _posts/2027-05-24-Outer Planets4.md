<div align="center"><mark><span style="color: blue;">In astronomy, the term outer planets refers to the planets that are farther from the Sun than the asteroid belt. While Jupiter and Saturn have been known since ancient times, the discovery of Uranus and Neptune required modern technology and mathematics. Mars is not an outer planet, even though it is farther from the Sun than Earth.</span></mark></div>

><span style="color: white">**The message on the Voyagers**</span>

The Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 each have an identical gramophone record on board with a message in image and sound. It is an example of simplex information to which there is probably no answer.  

Those Golden Records are (30 cm) gold-plated copper LPs. They are designed to last extremely long in space. They are attached to the outside at the back to protect them from the radiation on the go. They are also covered by a protective aluminum cover.

These plates are intended to be a time capsule and a universal message from humanity for any extraterrestrial intelligences that the probes might find in the distant future.

The content was selected by a committee led by the famous astronomer Carl Sagan (the driving force behind the SETI project or Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence) and Frank Drake (who gave his name to the Drake equation that allows calculating the probability of intelligent life in our Milky Way galaxy). That content is a cross-section of life and culture on earth. 12 copies were made, of which 10 are still on earth:
* Greetings in 55 different human languages including in English: "Warm greetings to everyone". There are also fragments from the whale and dolphin language.
* A collection of natural sounds: such as the rustling of the wind, thunder, waves, birds, animals (including laughing hyenas and elephants) and human sounds (footsteps, a kiss, a crying baby).
* A diverse selection of 90 minutes of music from all over the world, ranging from classical Western music (Bach, Beethoven, Mozart) to Eastern and ethnic music (Indian chants, Javanese percussion instruments, a Pygmy song).
* 115 images, encoded as analogue signals, of people, animals, landscapes and human anatomy (including genitals).

The full content of the album can be listened to and viewed on Voyager Golden Record.

Addition/Note:
* The message is an act of hope. She embodies the idea that, even if humanity were to become extinct, these plates would still travel through the galaxy for billions of years as a silent testimony to our existence.
* Their information is analogue which implies a lot of limitations.
* At that time, the 70s, there was no Compact Disk. This has to be scanned with a laser beam and of course offers many more possibilities because the stored information is digital.
* There were also no download packages of a few Gigabytes that could be read via a PC and that can display HD video images.

So the record is under an aluminum protective cover and on that cover are diagrams and instructions engraved to explain to any aliens how the record should be played and where it comes from (see below). Again, the location of pulsars is used to explain where our earth is. The bottom of the plate itself contains the concentric grooves that have to be scanned by the supplied needle. The diamond needle (stylus) is already in a ceramic holder (cartridge), together they form the crucial gramophone element that is also included and that is also stored under the aluminum cover (cover). The turntable itself is not provided.

><span style="color: white">**In detail > The right half explains how the decoding should take place**</span>

The record contains all kinds of analog sounds. It is known that the vibration of the needle will create a sound wave that only needs to be amplified to be heard better. In fact, one can hear it more or less without amplification, so this is not really a difficult job.

The record contains all kinds of analogue coded images. The following diagrams explain how to reconstruct them from the audio signal. It requires special decoding and that is no easy task. These are the steps that need to be taken:
* Just like a regular record player, the needle follows the spiral groove of the record.
* The vibrations of the needle are converted into an electrical signal.
* The electrical signal obtained is not a standard audio signal. It is an encrypted video signal.
* This video signal must be entered into a specialized electronic decoder. Again, this is not a standard feature of a normal turntable.
